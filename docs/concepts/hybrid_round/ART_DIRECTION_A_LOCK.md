# AXEHOLD — Living Illustrated Board Game

Status: selected direction. This document supersedes Concept I as the visual target.

## Player promise

Daylight feels warm, readable, and inviting. The player gathers, carries, and visibly improves a small home in the wilderness. Night preserves the same illustrated language but turns the outer forest cold and threatening while the Hearth remains a warm safe centre.

## Production ratio

- 70%: clarity and directness of the original flat prototype;
- 30%: authored illustration, animation, material response, and atmosphere;
- 0%: full-screen matte paintings used as gameplay backgrounds.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. Every interactive object reads as a separate high-quality game piece.
2. Ground, paths, water, vegetation, characters, and structures share one camera and scale.
3. The Wanderer keeps one silhouette and one set of proportions in every animation.
4. Movement is animated through feet, cloak, torso, and equipment—not whole-sprite bobbing.
5. An action must be understandable without HUD text before extra explanation is added.
6. Day is cosy, not childish; night is tense, not visually oppressive.
7. The first playable proof contains no enemies or timer. It proves the core tactile loop first.

## First playable proof

`move → chop → collect → carry → deliver → build`

Acceptance criteria:

- one-thumb tap movement works at 390×844;
- the hero is a large, stable, recognisable playing piece;
- tree hits have anticipation, contact, hit-stop, recoil, chips, and visible damage;
- three wood tokens travel to the backpack, then from the backpack to the build site;
- the first tower stage appears as a visible world change;
- no collage seams, sprite morphing, mismatched perspective, or explanatory clutter.
